We follow cloud native design, where the business functionality has segregated into small, independent Microservices.


What we have is cloud-native in the sense that we use microservices and async processing, and we separate reads into Redis. But we still 
share a single BRM system-of-record database across services, which introduces coupling. So architecturally it’s closer to CQRS with a 
shared database, and it has distributed-monolith risks. A more mature cloud-native microservice approach would move toward service-owned
data stores and event-driven integration

Is your system cloud-native: 
  Operationally, yes — we use microservices, async processing, and managed stores. But from a data-ownership standpoint, we still have a 
shared system-of-record database, which introduces coupling. So architecturally it’s a step toward cloud-native, but not fully aligned with
database-per-service principles.
